About this site

The companion to Quantum Mechanics I — the simplest quantum systems, from first principles.

What is here

2 lectures released so far, with 5 definitions, 10 numbered results, 8 proofs, 2 worked examples and 5 figures. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every theorem, equation and figure number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

This is a first course in quantum mechanics. It meets the simplest quantum systems one at a time, and for each it answers the same four questions: what are its states, what are its observables, how does it evolve, and what does a measurement give? It starts with the qubit, goes on to systems with finitely many levels, then to a particle in a box, on a line and in a well, and ends with two particles, identical or not.

It is written for students who know linear algebra and no quantum mechanics. Classical mechanics helps but is not needed. The notes are the lectured core and nothing else: each lecture is what is taught in one 90-minute slot. One exercise sheet per lecture is written after the lectures, and will appear here lecture by lecture. The course Quantum Mechanics (the second course) builds on this one.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. Every formula, sign and number the notes print is checked by a computer-algebra script before it is printed.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. This site is always the questions-only edition: worked solutions are done in the tutorial, which is where the learning in them actually happens.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published.

Your privacy

There is no account, no login, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your theme choice, and the presentation view's settings and place in each lecture, are remembered in your own browser; nothing else is stored.

Found a mistake?

Please report it. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable — bring it to a lecture or tutorial and it will be fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
